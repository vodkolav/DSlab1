What role do neural networks play in optimizing 3D propellant grain geometries?
Neural networks play a transformative role in optimizing 3D propellant grain geometries by shifting the design paradigm from traditional, parameter-driven methods to automated, data-driven, and shape-flexible approaches. Their primary functions include defining irregular grain geometries, acting as rapid surrogate models for internal ballistics, and directly solving the complex physics governing combustion and regression.
1. Representation of Grain Geometry (Shape Optimization)
One of the most significant roles of neural networks is enabling reverse design, where the optimal grain shape is generated to match a predefined performance curve (such as pressure-time or thrust-time), rather than the traditional forward method of predicting performance from a fixed shape.
• Phase Field Parameterization: Instead of defining a grain using limited geometric parameters (e.g., the angle of a star or the radius of a slot), a Feedforward Neural Network (FNN) can be used to construct a "phase field" function. This function, ϕ(x,y), implicitly defines the distribution of the propellant (solid vs. gas cavity) across the domain .
• Freedom from Geometric Constraints: By using a neural network to define the grain's topology, designers are no longer restricted to standard shapes like stars or wagon wheels. The network allows for the evolution of highly irregular, "brand-new" grain shapes—such as complex dendritic structures—that better match specific dual-thrust or neutral burning requirements.
• Evolutionary Optimization: The hyperparameters (weights and biases) of the neural network are optimized using evolutionary algorithms, such as Genetic Algorithms (GA). As the GA updates the network parameters, the grain geometry evolves until its predicted ballistic performance matches the target curve.
2. Surrogate Modeling for Rapid Performance Prediction
Optimizing 3D grains requires evaluating thousands of potential designs, which is computationally prohibitive using high-fidelity Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) or finite element methods. Neural networks serve as surrogate models to drastically reduce this computational cost.
• Replacing Internal Ballistics Codes: Neural networks can be trained on datasets generated by 1D or 3D internal ballistics codes (e.g., Monte Carlo simulations of tapered grains). Once trained, these networks can predict complex performance metrics—such as maximum thrust, average thrust, and total impulse—almost instantaneously.
• Topology Generation: Advanced architectures like Conditional Wasserstein Generative Adversarial Networks (CWGAN) are used to generate quasi-optimal grain structures. Unlike iterative optimization, which can take minutes or hours, a trained CWGAN can generate an optimized structure satisfying specific volume constraints in under 0.5 seconds.
• Tapered Grain Analysis: For complex geometries involving tapered grains (where the port geometry changes longitudinally), neural networks have proven effective at predicting the full thrust-time profile, capturing the non-linear effects of geometric tapering that simple analytical models might miss.
3. Physics-Informed Solvers (PINNs)
Beyond standard data-driven modeling, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) integrate the governing physical laws directly into the network's training process, allowing them to solve the partial differential equations (PDEs) associated with grain burn-back and combustion.
• Solving Burn-Back and Pyrolysis: PINNs can simulate the regression of the burning surface and the pyrolysis of the polymer matrix by solving coupled PDEs (such as the Eikonal equation for front propagation and heat transfer equations) without requiring a mesh. This allows for the prediction of temperature distributions and char formation patterns within the grain material.
• Combustion Dynamics: PINNs are also employed to predict combustion dynamics, including pressure oscillations and flame front instabilities, by integrating mass, momentum, and energy conservation laws into the loss function. This approach maintains high physical fidelity while being significantly faster than traditional CFD solvers (e.g., 0.5 seconds vs. 12 hours).

1. What is "Web Burned"?
In the context of the curve Ab​−w (Burning Area vs. Web Burned), the "web burned" (often referred to as burned web thickness) represents the cumulative distance the burning surface has regressed (moved) perpendicular to itself at a given point in the combustion process,.
• Geometric Definition: It is a measure of the propellant thickness that has been consumed. In many analytical models, this is denoted as the variable y (burn distance) or w,.
• Decoupling Physics from Geometry: The transformation of a pressure-time (pc​−t) curve into an area-web (Ab​−w) curve allows designers to separate the grain's geometry from the complex internal ballistics (such as burn rate pressure dependence). While pressure depends on time and burn rate, the "Burning Area vs. Web Burned" relationship is purely geometric, describing how much surface area exists after the flame has traveled a specific distance into the propellant.
• Burn Phases: As the "web burned" increases, the grain geometry may shift between different "phases" (e.g., from a star shape to a slotted tube, or when a star point burns out and hits the casing wall), which drastically changes the burning surface area,.
2. What is the "Phase Field" Method?
The Phase Field method is a technique used to mathematically represent the shape and topology of the propellant grain using a continuous scalar function, rather than using explicit geometric parameters (like lines, arcs, or radii),.
• How it Works: A function, denoted as ϕ(x,y), is defined over the entire design domain (the cross-section of the motor). This function assigns a value to every coordinate to distinguish between the propellant and the empty space (gas cavity):
    ◦ Solid Propellant: ϕ(x,y)>0.
    ◦ Gas/Cavity: ϕ(x,y)<0.
    ◦ Burning Interface: ϕ(x,y)=0 (The zero-level set represents the actual surface of the grain).
• Role of Neural Networks: Instead of using traditional basis functions, a Feedforward Neural Network (FNN) can be used to approximate this function ϕ(x,y). The network takes spatial coordinates (x,y) as inputs and outputs the phase field value, effectively defining the grain's shape,.
• Advantages for Optimization:
    ◦ Fixed Mesh: This method allows for "gas-solid unified burn-back analysis" on a fixed mesh. As the grain burns or evolves during optimization, the mesh does not need to be regenerated; only the values of ϕ change,.
    ◦ Shape Freedom: Because the shape is defined by a field rather than rigid parameters (like "number of star points"), the optimizer can create highly irregular, brand-new topologies (such as complex dendritic shapes) that traditional parameter-driven methods cannot easily describe,.

The term "web" in solid rocket motor design does not allude to a spider web or a net. Instead, it refers to the structural thickness of the solid propellant material between the internal burning surface and the outer casing (or the point where the geometry changes).
Based on the sources, "web" is used as a standard engineering term for a solid wall or thickness of material:
• Definition of Thickness: The "web thickness" (denoted as w) is strictly defined as a linear measurement of the propellant's depth. For example, in a "Star Grain" configuration, the web thickness w is the distance from the tip of the star to the outer wall of the motor.
• Web Burned: Consequently, the "web burned" (or burned web thickness) tracks the cumulative distance the burning surface has regressed (moved) through that solid propellant thickness over time.
• Geometric Phases: In complex shapes like star grains, the "web" is often divided into phases (e.g., Web1 and Web2). Web1 might represent the thickness burnt while the star shape is preserved, and Web2 represents the remaining thickness after the star points have burned away and the flame front becomes circular.
Analogy: Think of the "web" of an I-beam or a rail track—the solid vertical section connecting the top and bottom flanges. Similarly, in a rocket grain, the "web" is the solid "wall" of fuel. "Web burned" is simply a measure of how much of that wall thickness has been consumed by the fire.
